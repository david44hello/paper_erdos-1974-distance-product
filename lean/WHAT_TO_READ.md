# What to read in the distance-product proof

Start with [the theorem audit](AUDIT.md). Each row links to the declaration and names any analytic facts supplied as hypotheses. [The paper correspondence](CLAIM_MAP.tsv) locates each declaration in the paper or identifies its role as a helper. [The audit notes](AUDIT_NOTES.md) explain the hypotheses of the main results.

| Question | Where to read |
|---|---|
| Which placements maximize the volume of the ordinary distance-product solid? | In every dimension d ≥ 3, take any n ≥ 1 marks and the set of points whose product of distances to the marks is at most 1. Under the six named capacity and equilibrium-measure hypotheses, this set has finite volume at most the volume of the unit d-ball. Equality holds exactly when all marks coincide. [Full statement and proof](ErdosVolume/Paper/TwoMarkSlices.lean#L405) |
| What supplies the planar maximum in the title and table? | Pólya’s Satz II supplies the all-degree planar area bound and equality case. The tagged Lean volume theorem for arbitrary numbers of marks begins in dimension three. [Pólya, p. 280](https://archive.org/download/sitzungsbericht1928preu/page/n311_w900.jpg) |
| What does the bound give in three dimensions? | The maximum is 4π/3 for every n ≥ 1, attained exactly when all marks coincide. [Three-dimensional specialization](ErdosVolume/Paper/Statements.lean#L116) |
| Which planar result does the two-mark slice proof use? | Lean assumes the open unit sublevel from Erdős–Herzog–Piranian (1958), p. 134. Pólya’s original zweite Mitteilung (1928), Satz II, pp. 280–282, states the closed bound for every positive threshold. For two marks, Lean proves the boundary has area zero and rescales the result. [Lean hypothesis](ErdosVolume/Paper/TwoMarkSlices.lean#L112) · [Erdős–Herzog–Piranian, p. 134](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1958-05.pdf#page=10) · [DOI 10.1007/BF02790232](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02790232) · [Pólya, p. 280](https://archive.org/download/sitzungsbericht1928preu/page/n311_w900.jpg) |
| How does the capacity argument bound volume and characterize equality? | `volume_capacity` in [Capacity.lean](ErdosVolume/Capacity.lean) uses six named capacity and equilibrium-measure facts. The separate flux argument, `Flux.direct_bound_eq`, uses eight named facts in [Flux.lean](ErdosVolume/Flux.lean). |
| How long can the region of two marks on a line be? | Its length is at most 2√2 and equality holds precisely at separation two. The region is one interval for separations at most two and two disjoint intervals for larger separations. [Formulas and sharp bound](ErdosVolume/Paper/PairGeometry.lean#L148) |
| Which part of the inradius estimate is sharp? | [Sharpness.lean](ErdosVolume/Sharpness.lean) gives the two-mark ratio limit in `pair_ratio_limit` and the counterexamples to every smaller coefficient in `sharp_coefficient`. |
| How general is the upper bound? | `Paper.uniform_upper_given` in [Conditional.lean](ErdosVolume/Paper/Conditional.lean) covers all compactly supported source probabilities at each positive threshold under its seven named premises. |
| What happens along a sequence of positive deficits tending to zero? | At unit threshold the upper limit of fractional inradius loss divided by deficit is at most one. The trial-cost estimate first holds at each sufficiently small fixed annulus width δ, with positive denominator 1/6 − Cδ. Both statements use the same seven stability hypotheses. [Inradius limit](ErdosVolume/Paper/StabilityLimits.lean#L36) · [Fixed-δ cost bound](ErdosVolume/Paper/StabilityLimits.lean#L100) |
| What does the Discussion say at a fixed volume cutoff? | In dimension three, among all admissible laws whose unit-threshold volume is at least (1−ε) times the unit-ball volume, the infimum of the inradius is 1−ε+o(ε). This includes laws of zero deficit and does not require an attained infimum. [Zero-deficit equality](ErdosVolume/Paper/Discussion.lean#L82) · [Sharp infimum](ErdosVolume/Paper/Discussion.lean#L212) |
| Which statements are open? | The five rows marked OPEN in [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md) are the unconditional statements `Paper.finite_volume`, `Paper.erdos_product`, `Paper.first_order_radius`, `uniform_upper` and `sharp_stability`. |

## Results taken as given

Theorem 1.1 has two proofs. The capacity proof takes six named hypotheses and the flux proof takes eight. Theorem 2.1 takes seven, four of which also occur in the capacity proof. Together these three theorem statements use 17 distinct hypotheses. The final four entries identify a separate geometric argument, a helper, and two intermediate results derived in Lean. The audit gives the full list of declarations that use each hypothesis. [Full theorem types and their inputs](AUDIT.md)

## Verification of the paper proof

For the paper release, Lean’s native comparator accepted all 170 finished declarations against their stated types after a clean build. Of those declarations, 124 are proved and 46 are proved under the named hypotheses listed in the audit. The five unconditional statements marked OPEN were excluded from the comparison.

The five additional checkers `leanchecker-paranoid`, `lean4lean`, `nanoda`, `con-leche`, and `con-ron` accepted the same exported proof. As a negative control, deleting one line from the export caused `leanchecker-paranoid` to reject it because a referenced expression was missing.

The accepted paper-proof export was 519,450,614 bytes. Its SHA-256 digest is `5e362eb03da065eff74acd20df55d805e37b54c596e06ee1ffc13acbc2fd563a`.
